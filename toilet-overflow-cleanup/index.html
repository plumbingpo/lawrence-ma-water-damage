Dealing With Toilet Overflow Cleanup in Lawrence

It is tempting to grab a mop after a toilet overflows, yet the water never stays on top of the tile. The gap at the drain pipe, the wall and the space under the baseboard all take water in. What matters most is what was in it. If it came from the tank or the supply line, it is clean. Water that came up through the bowl is not, and it has to be handled as contaminated.

Our Lawrence crew starts by identifying the water category, because that sets the rules for everything after. With clean water, the job is extraction followed by drying. For gray or black water we wear protective gear, remove soaked porous items such as carpet pad, and disinfect each surface the water reached. Finally we meter the subfloor, the bottoms of the walls and the space underneath, so no moisture gets left behind.

Do This First

Before We Arrive

  1. Stop the WaterFind the shutoff valve behind the toilet and turn it clockwise as far as it goes. When the valve is frozen, remove the lid of the tank and hold the float up so filling stops.
  2. Do Not Flush AgainDo not flush again or run sinks, showers or the washer. If the drain line is blocked, any water you send down can come back up.
  3. Avoid Dirty WaterWhen there is waste in the water or it came out of the bowl, keep pets and children away, and put on waterproof shoes and gloves before stepping in it.
  4. Check the Room BelowIf the toilet is upstairs, go check the ceiling directly below it. A ceiling that sags or drips means turning off that room's power and moving your things clear.
  5. Contain Clean WaterUse old towels to keep clean water from reaching carpet or hallways. Anything contaminated is a job for people with proper gear and disinfecting products.
Common Causes

Why This Happens

  • A stuck flapper or fill valve that keeps the tank running without stopping.
  • A clog in the trap or drain line that sends bowl water over the rim.
  • A main sewer line blockage that backs waste up through the lowest toilet.
  • Roots growing into the sewer line, or a section that has collapsed, slowing all the drains.
  • Waste water with nowhere to go because the septic tank is full or the drain field is saturated.
If It Is Left

What Happens if You Wait

Baseboard and Door Trim Damage

Baseboards and door casings sit right at floor level and soak up water from the bottom edge. Since trim is usually fiberboard, it puffs up, cracks apart and stays deformed.

Vanity and Drywall Wicking

Water spreads to the vanity toe kick and the lower edge of the drywall, where it wicks upward. The wall can be wet several inches above the floor while the paint still looks fine.

Bacterial Contamination

Bowl and drain water leaves bacteria in carpet, pad, baseboards and drywall. Because disinfecting porous material is not reliable, it usually has to come out.

Lingering Odor

When contaminated water dries under flooring or in the base of a wall, the smell comes back in humid weather and stays until that material is removed.

Insurance

Insurance and Toilet Overflow Cleanup

A sudden and accidental overflow, for example from an unexpected clog or a fill valve that failed, is covered by many homeowners policies. Sewer or drain backup is often excluded without a backup endorsement on the policy, and an overflow from a blocked main line can fall under that exclusion. Damage from a toilet that leaked at the base for months is usually not covered. To learn which of these applies in your case, check the policy or phone your agent.
