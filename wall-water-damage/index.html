Wall and Drywall Water Damage in Lawrence

Think of drywall as a wick. Paper and gypsum pull water up from the floor line, so the wet zone frequently ends a foot or more past the highest point the water itself touched. Insulation and the rear of the panel can be wet through while the paint out front shows nothing unusual. Looks cannot be trusted here, so every wall gets tested with a meter.

Not every wet wall has to be torn out. Drywall that got wet with clean water and is still firm can often be dried in place, sometimes by removing the baseboard and moving air through small holes into the cavity. When the wall is soft, crumbling, moldy or was soaked by dirty water, we make a flood cut, a straight line above the damage, and remove everything below it.

Warning Signs

  • Fasteners and joint lines becoming visible on a once smooth wall
  • Soft or crumbly spots you notice when pushing lightly on the wall
  • Staining in a horizontal band just above the baseboard
  • Cool, slightly clammy drywall next to walls at normal temperature
  • Door casing and trim joints separating from the drywall
  • Carpet edges or floorboards wet and discolored right next to the wall
The Clock

How the Damage Progresses

The first few hours

Water Starts Climbing

Moisture enters where the panel meets the floor and wicks upward from there. Baseboards and the bottom plate take on moisture.

Hours 24 to 48

Wall Swells

You may see blistered paint and swollen trim as the damp area moves up the wall. Inside the cavity, the paper face becomes a place mold can begin.

Day 3 to 7

Panels Lose Strength

Paper peels from a core that is no longer firm. A musty odor grows stronger while the batts settle and mold covers more of the unseen face.

Past one week

Damage Reaches the Studs

By this point the studs and plates have been damp long enough for mold and early rot. The amount of removal and reconstruction grows considerably.

Our Process

How We Handle Wall and Drywall Water Damage

1

Map the Moisture

Using meter readings and thermal images, our team outlines where the water went, upward and sideways, on both faces of the wall and in neighboring rooms.

2

Decide What Stays

Our call on each section, dry it or take it out, depends on water category, exposure time and the condition of the panel, and we explain it in plain terms.

3

Open Up or Air Out

Walls we are keeping have their baseboards removed so air can be pushed into the cavity. Walls that cannot be kept get a flood cut along a level line, with the wet insulation pulled.

4

Dry and Rebuild

Air movers and dehumidifiers run until studs and remaining drywall read dry, typically 3 to 5 days. After that comes insulation, new drywall hung and finished, trim put back and paint.

Behind the Surface

The Damage You Do Not See

Moisture in Exterior Walls

Water in a perimeter wall may reach the sheathing on the far side of the cavity. It gets a moisture reading prior to closing, since it holds dampness a long time.

Growth Behind the Paint

The back of the drywall is dark, still and lined with paper, which is ideal for mold. You can have a clean looking painted surface with heavy growth right behind it.

Wet Outlets and Wiring

Receptacles and cables sit low in the wall, right where water collects. Do not use outlets on a wet wall until the circuit has been checked.

Insulation Holding Water

Batts of fiberglass absorb water and keep it pressed to the framing. Drying from inside the room is not enough when the insulation is soaked.
